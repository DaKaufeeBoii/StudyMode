#1. Unit 3 (Q4): Convolution & Pooling as an "Infinitely Strong Prior" Hard
The Trap: Saying convolution is just sliding a filter and pooling takes the maximum.
The Aha! Insight: In Bayesian probability, an infinitely strong prior gives zero probability to certain functions. By choosing a CNN over an MLP, we permanently forbid long-range direct connections (weights strictly 0) and force weights across space to be identical (parameter sharing). It fails on position-sensitive tasks (like face landmarking where eyes are always at the top).
#2. Unit 4 (Q7): LSTM Mathematical Solution to Vanishing Gradient (CEC) Hard
The Trap: Memorizing gate names without writing the Jacobian.
The Aha! Insight: In standard RNNs, gradients multiply repeatedly by $W_{hh}$, causing exponential decay ($0.9^{50} \approx 0$). In LSTM, the backpropagation flows along the Cell State $C_t = f_t \odot C_{t-1} + i_t \odot \tilde{C}_t$. The derivative is $\frac{\partial C_t}{\partial C_{t-1}} = f_t$. Gradients flow backward by simple addition without repeated matrix multiplication!
#3. Unit 2 (Q9): Why $L_1$ Regularization Forces Exact Zero (Sparsity) vs $L_2$ Hard
The Trap: Saying "L1 is Lasso and L2 is Ridge" without the math or geometry.
The Aha! Insight: The derivative of $L_2$ is $\alpha w$. As $w \to 0$, the penalty force also shrinks to 0 (it never reaches pure 0). The derivative of $L_1$ is $\alpha \cdot \text{sign}(w)$, which is a constant push that kicks small weights straight into absolute 0. Geometrically, the $L_1$ constraint region is a diamond with sharp corners on the coordinate axes.
#4. Unit 4 (Q11): Echo State Networks (ESNs) & Spectral Radius Hard
The Trap: Assuming recurrent weights must be trained with backpropagation.
The Aha! Insight: ESNs freeze a massive, random recurrent reservoir and never backpropagate through it! Only the linear output layer is trained using one-shot linear regression. For the network to have a fading memory ("Echo State Property"), the spectral radius $\rho(W_{res})$ must be $< 1.0$ so internal echoes die out over time rather than exploding into chaos.
#5. Unit 1 (Q5): Vector-Matrix Backpropagation Derivation Hard
The Trap: Writing 1D scalar chain rule and losing 6 marks for missing layer matrices.
The Aha! Insight: Backpropagation is simply two matrix equations repeated backwards: (1) Error Vector: $\delta^{(l)} = ((W^{(l+1)})^T \delta^{(l+1)}) \odot \sigma'(z^{(l)})$, and (2) Weight Gradient: $\frac{\partial J}{\partial W^{(l)}} = \delta^{(l)} (a^{(l-1)})^T$.
